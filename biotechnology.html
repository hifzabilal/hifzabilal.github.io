02 · Resist

Research Intern, Agricultural Biotechnology

Agricultural Biotechnology Research Institute (ABRI), Ayub Agricultural Research Institute, Faisalabad
13/05/2026 – 13/08/2026 · part-time

OverviewPlant PathologyBiotechnologyEntomologyNurseries

Context

ABRI uses DNA markers, genetic engineering and micropropagation to support crop breeding and disease-free planting material. Here I worked on the genetics of resistance, especially rust resistance in wheat.

What I did

  • Ran SSR-marker PCR for the wheat rust resistance genes Lr19/Sr25, Lr28 and Lr34 on batches of up to 170 samples, and resolved products on agarose and polyacrylamide gels.
  • Extracted DNA from wheat leaf tissue by the CTAB method, extracted RNA, and quantified nucleic acids with a NanoDrop spectrophotometer.
  • Performed quantitative real-time PCR.
  • Prepared MS, half-strength MS and silver nitrate media and carried out tissue culture of stevia, paulownia and tea plant. Repeated contamination taught me to refine every step of aseptic technique.
  • Gained exposure to genetic transformation, CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing, DNA fingerprinting, confocal and fluorescence microscopy, Western blotting and bioinformatics.

Methods

  • CTAB DNA extraction
  • RNA extraction
  • NanoDrop quantification
  • PCR
  • SSR markers
  • RT-qPCR
  • Agarose gel
  • PAGE
  • MS media
  • Plant tissue culture

From the lab and field

What it taught me

Seeing CRISPR/Cas9 and marker-assisted selection showed me that a plant's own genes can reduce pesticide use. It also raised a harder question, because resistance genes can break down as pathogens evolve. Since many crop problems are caused or spread by insects, I then moved to entomology.
